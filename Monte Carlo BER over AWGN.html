Experiment 12 — Monte Carlo BER over AWGN

Symbol-level simulation of BPSK, QPSK, coherent orthogonal BFSK, 8-PSK, 16-QAM and 64-QAM with Gray labeling and minimum-distance detection. Every Eb/N0 point runs until the error target or the bit limit is reached, whichever comes first. Points with zero errors are shown as upper bounds, never as BER = 0.

1 · Monte Carlo settings

Idle.
Expected: simulated markers fall on the theoretical lines wherever the line is valid and enough errors were counted (≥ 100 errors gives roughly ±10% relative uncertainty). Curves shift right with modulation order at a given BER, BFSK sits 3 dB to the right of BPSK, and the 8-PSK/16-QAM/64-QAM theory curves are high-SNR approximations that overestimate BER at low Eb/N0. At the highest Eb/N0 values the budget of 106 bits cannot resolve BER below about 3×10−6: those points must appear as upper bounds.

2 · Eb/N0 → noise variance, with numerical check

Chain used by the simulator: Es = average energy of the normalized constellation (= 1), Eb = Es/k with k = log2M bits per symbol, N0 = Eb/(Eb/N0)linear, complex noise variance σ² = N0 (so N0/2 per real dimension). The last column is the variance actually measured on the noise added inside the simulator.

5 · Mandatory validation

(a) With σ = 0 and at Eb/N0 = 40 dB the detector must return the transmitted bits exactly. (b) At Eb/N0 = −30 dB the received sample carries essentially no information about the sent symbol, so each bit is wrong with probability 1/2 regardless of constellation or mapping.
